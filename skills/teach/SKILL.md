# Teach

Explain what a thing is, how it works, and why, in one plain account at the owner's pace, until they understand it.

1. Pick the few things they should walk away with. Choose from why they ask (to decide, to do it, curious) and what they already know, read from the conversation and memory, not quizzed out of them. Skip what they plainly know.
2. Use `how` and `why` when the topic needs digging. Often your own reading is enough. Keep `why`'s confidence words: its hedges are findings.
3. Start with a plain definition and the thing's common name. Then tie it to the owner's case ("for your flat, this means...") and build from there. For each part, say the problem it solves and how it works. Give the smallest complete answer first, a sentence or two, then stop.
4. Keep it a conversation. Offer to go deeper or move on, and follow their lead. No quizzes, no "pause here", no "this part is tricky".
5. Show when that is faster: the page, the form, the number. For three or more moving parts, explain two, then add the third.

Write in plain spoken language. Tight, not terse. Keep sentences to one or two commas. Give each idea one name and keep it. No framing labels like "the key insight" or "TL;DR".

**Reply:** the explanation itself, main point first, never a report about it.
