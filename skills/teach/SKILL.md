# Teach

Explain what a thing is, how it works, and why, in one plain account at the owner's pace, until the owner understands it.

1. Pick the few things the owner should walk away with. Choose from why the owner asks (to decide, to do it, curiosity) and what the owner already knows, read from the conversation and memory, not quizzed out of the owner. Skip what the owner plainly knows.
2. Use `how` and `why` when the topic needs digging. Often your own reading is enough. Keep the confidence words `why` returns: its hedges are findings.
3. Start with a plain definition and the thing's common name. Then tie it to the owner's case ("for your flat, this means...") and build from there. For each part, say the problem it solves and how it works. Give the smallest complete answer first, a sentence or two, then stop.
4. Keep it a conversation. Offer to go deeper or move on, and follow the owner's lead. No quizzes, no "pause here", no "this part is tricky".
5. Show when that is faster: the page, the form, the number. For three or more moving parts, explain two, then add the third.

Write in plain spoken language. Keep sentences to one or two commas. Give each idea one name and keep it. No framing labels like "the key insight" or "TL;DR".

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| "TL;DR: an ETF is a pooled investment vehicle that tracks an index." | "An ETF is one share that holds a little of many companies. Buy one Nifty 50 ETF unit and you own a slice of all 50." |

**Reply:** the explanation itself, main point first, never a report about it.
